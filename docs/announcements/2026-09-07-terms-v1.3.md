# Terms & Privacy v1.3 — effective 2026-09-07

terakota's License & Terms (EULA), Privacy Notice, and Security Policy move to
**version 1.3, effective 2026-09-07**. The live copies are at
[terakota.io/terms](https://terakota.io/terms),
[terakota.io/privacy](https://terakota.io/privacy), and
[terakota.io/security](https://terakota.io/security); the same texts are in this
repository under [docs/legal/](../legal/) and [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md).

## Why

Terakota runs a hosted delivery spine for tenants we onboard, and its control
panel is reachable two ways: in the browser at `app.terakota.io`, and — from the
upcoming **v1.8.0** release — from the `terakota` binary. The bridge is optional
and it is two commands you run yourself: `terakota login` signs the binary in to
your terakota account through our sign-in provider, in your browser, and
`terakota link` binds one local company to one hosted tenant you belong to.
Nothing signs in on its own. A binary that has not signed in behaves exactly as
it does today.

That bridge adds two things to the set of hosts the Software may contact — our
sign-in host, run for us by Auth0, and our control-plane API — so the terms name
them before the release ships. Both the EULA and the
Privacy Notice hold us to that order: adding a connection to a service of ours
means changing them first, published and announced before it takes effect.

## What changed

- **EULA 1.3 — Section 4's enumerated closed set grows from two classes to
  three.** It now names our sign-in host, `dev-bo1prweh.us.auth0.com`, run for
  us by our sign-in provider (Auth0); our control-plane API at
  `app.terakota.io/api/v1`; and every command that may call either, each poll of
  a running `events-tail` counted as one call. It also states the second — and
  last — place the Software talks to a host in that list without you asking for
  that particular call: when a command finds the sign-in access token expired,
  it renews the token against the sign-in host before the read, inside the
  command you ran and nowhere else. The list stays closed: a release may carry
  fewer of those commands than the Section lists, and it never carries a call
  the Section does not.
- **EULA 1.3 — Section 2 describes the control-panel mode and its limits.**
  After sign-in and link, the `platform-*` commands and `events-tail` read our
  own delivery metadata for the linked tenant: which topics exist, which
  subscriptions route them and to which named destination, queue counts, held
  and dead-lettered deliveries (never their content), and an index of delivered
  events that has no payload column. A call sends us the linked tenant, the
  command, and the command's own inputs — never a vendor credential, a query, a
  result, a receipt, or your local company id, and nothing you type as
  `--intent` leaves your machine. The sentence that used to say the Software
  makes no call to any host of ours at all is now conditioned on not having
  signed in.
- **EULA 1.3 — Section 8 separates signing out from closing an account.**
  `terakota logout` revokes the sign-in and deletes the tokens from your
  machine, and does nothing else; closing the account is still a written request
  to contact@bilans.io. Closing it closes the control panel to you, and routing
  changes already made on a tenant stay as they are until an operator or another
  member changes them. The preamble and Sections 6 and 9 put the control panel
  under the Portal Account Terms with the same precedence the account and the
  connect service already had.
- **Privacy Notice 1.3 — the lede gains a third mode, and §1 mirrors the closed
  set.** A new §1 paragraph states what a control-plane call sends and what
  comes back. The account-free "no call at all" sentence is conditioned on not
  having signed in.
- **Privacy Notice 1.3 — §3a is retitled and states what the panel records.**
  Every routing change writes one append-only row in our engine store: when,
  which account id (an internal identifier, not your email), which tenant, which
  action, a digest of the routing before and after, and the database login it
  ran under. From terakota `v1.8.0`, each control-plane read and each
  `events-tail` poll is recorded separately and more briefly — one line in a
  read log in our control store, carrying your account id, the tenant, which
  read and the time, with no inputs and no results. **We delete read-log lines
  after 90 days**, and closing your account deletes yours in the same step that
  clears your email and display name; while a tail runs, that is one line every
  few seconds, which is a record of when your machine was polling. The change
  rows are different: they are append-only and have no automatic expiry today,
  and erasure does not rewrite them — after closure they carry an account id
  that resolves to no one. §3a also adds the three ways an account comes to
  exist, the control panel as the account's fourth purpose, the sign-in tokens
  on your machine and how `logout` revokes them, and what `terakota link`
  writes locally.
- **Privacy Notice 1.3 — §3 names the panel and its `/api/v1` among our hosted
  surfaces; §5 names the new breach surface** (a tenant's routing, the audit
  rows, and the delivery-event index, all metadata, resting in our engine store
  behind one database role that sees only that tenant's rows); **§6 names the
  audit rows** among what we hold about you.
- **Security Policy 1.3 — the control panel is in scope.** The scope line names
  the panel and its API. Safe harbor gains a rule for it: test only against a
  tenant you are a member of, never another tenant's routing, audit rows, or
  event index (§2). §3's "nothing reports on its own" line records that a
  signed-in, linked binary calls the control plane from terakota `v1.8.0` — but
  only for a command you run, and a tail only while you leave it running; the
  one stated automatic exception (connect-service token refresh) stays one. §4
  adds a third boundary: the sign-in tokens on your machine can read, and later
  change, the routing of the tenants you belong to, so they are yours to
  protect. §5 records the panel's posture — switched on for the operator's own
  account only until we open it to other accounts, with an adversarial review of
  tenant isolation before the first one.

## What v1.8.0 carries, and what it does not

The terms are published ahead of the release. When v1.8.0 ships it carries the
sign-in and the reads:

- `terakota login`, `terakota logout`, `terakota account`, `terakota link`,
  `terakota unlink`;
- the six reads — `platform-status`, `platform-topics`,
  `platform-subscriptions`, `platform-queue-health`, `platform-quarantine`,
  `platform-dlq`;
- `events-tail`, which polls the delivery-event index every few seconds for as
  long as you leave it running, one call per poll;
- and the five MCP read tools — `platform_status_get`, `platform_topics_list`,
  `platform_subscriptions_list`, `platform_queue_health_get`,
  `platform_events_list`. No MCP tool signs in, links, changes routing, or runs
  a tail.

**It does not carry the four change commands.** `platform-subscribe`,
`platform-unsubscribe`, `platform-replay` and `platform-catchall-replay` appear
in EULA §4's list as the ceiling that list sets — no release may make a call the
Section does not name — and they are not in v1.8.0. **No v1.8.0 binary can
change a tenant's delivery routing.** Routing changes are made in the browser
panel today; when the binary's change commands ship they are receipted on the
linked company's chain, and the terms already say so.

Which of these touch a host, and what each leaves behind:

- `terakota account` reads the entry in your local keystore and contacts
  nobody. It leaves no record with us.
- `terakota login` and `terakota logout` talk to the sign-in host only. Login
  returns an access token that expires within an hour and a refresh token that
  rotates on every use, both stored only in your local keystore; logout revokes
  the refresh token and deletes both.
- `terakota link` makes one `platform-status` read to check your membership —
  one call, and the one read-log line every read leaves — then writes the link
  to a file on your machine. `terakota unlink` removes it and contacts nothing.
- The six reads, `events-tail` and the five MCP tools call
  `app.terakota.io/api/v1`. Each leaves one read-log line, kept 90 days.
- None of the reads and no tail poll record anything on your local receipt
  chain. The hosted read log is the record of them instead.
- The control panel and its API are switched on per account by us. Today that
  is the operator's own account only, so a signed-in account is not entitled to
  the panel until we grant it.

## What did not change

- No telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting — still zero, in any version,
  in any mode. Nothing reports on its own.
- Your business data still never reaches us. Your credentials, queries, results
  and receipt chains stay on your machine; no interface of ours can reach them.
- AppFolio, Dialpad, local reconciliation, receipts, `verify-receipts`, and
  QuickBooks against an Intuit sandbox company under your own registered Intuit
  application need no account and send us nothing. Signing in and linking change
  none of that: an AppFolio read runs the same way and sends the same nothing.
- Production QuickBooks is untouched — the same connect service, the same free
  account, the same eleven-field connection record, the same retention windows.
- A binary that has not signed in, or that has signed out, contacts no host of
  ours beyond the connect-service calls a production QuickBooks connection
  needs. With neither, it contacts no host of ours at all.
- The binaries remain read-only by construction toward your business systems.
